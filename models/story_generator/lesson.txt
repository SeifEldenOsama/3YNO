Water is always moving around our planet in a never-ending journey called the water cycle.
It starts when the sun heats up water in oceans, rivers, and lakes. This heat turns liquid
water into tiny invisible droplets of water vapor that float up into the sky — a process
called evaporation.

As water vapor rises higher into the sky, it cools down and turns back into tiny water
droplets that cluster together to form clouds. This process is called condensation. Clouds
are actually made of millions of tiny water droplets floating in the air.

When clouds collect too much water, the water falls back down to Earth as rain, snow, or
hail — this is called precipitation. The fallen water flows into rivers, lakes, and oceans,
or soaks into the ground. Then the sun heats it up again and the whole cycle starts over.

By learning about the water cycle, children understand that the water we drink today has
been traveling around Earth for millions of years, and that every drop of water is precious
and worth protecting.
