Plants are all around us — in forests, gardens, and parks — and they all begin their lives as tiny seeds on an incredible journey.
It starts when a seed falls from a plant onto the ground. Some seeds are carried by the wind, some float on water, and some are dropped by animals or birds. When a seed lands in soil with enough water and warmth, it begins to sprout — a process called germination. A tiny root pushes down into the soil while a small shoot pushes up toward the light.
As the shoot breaks through the surface, it unfolds its first leaves and begins to soak up sunlight. Plants use sunlight, water from the soil, and air to make their own food — this amazing process is called photosynthesis. The leaves act like tiny solar panels, capturing energy from the sun to help the plant grow bigger and stronger.
When the plant is fully grown, it produces flowers to attract bees and butterflies. These insects carry a fine yellow powder called pollen from flower to flower. When pollen reaches the right flower, it creates a new seed inside — this is called pollination. The seed is wrapped in a fruit or pod to protect it.
Eventually the fruit falls or is eaten, the seed is released, and the whole journey begins again. By learning about how plants grow, children understand that every tree, every flower, and every piece of fruit starts from something as small as a single seed — and that soil, sunlight, and water are precious gifts that make all life possible.
